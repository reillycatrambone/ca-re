## The activity determines the license

A real estate license gives permission to perform defined activities; it is not a general credential for every business connected with land. California ordinarily requires a broker license when a person, for compensation or in expectation of compensation, acts for another to sell, buy, lease, negotiate, solicit, or perform other statutorily defined real estate services. Arranging certain secured loans and selling business opportunities also fall within the law. One transaction can be enough. Calling a fee a consulting payment does not change the underlying activity.

An owner dealing with the owner's own property generally is not acting for another. Other exemptions exist for specified persons and functions, including certain court-appointed fiduciaries. Exemptions have boundaries: being an attorney, accountant, relative, or experienced investor does not create unrestricted authority to conduct a brokerage business. On the exam, identify who owns the property, whose interests the worker serves, what the worker actually does, and whether compensation is expected.

A salesperson performs licensed activity through an employing or responsible broker. Passing the examination alone is insufficient. The license must be issued and effective, and the necessary broker affiliation must exist before the salesperson begins licensed work. A salesperson cannot independently establish a brokerage merely by creating a company or calling the business a real estate team.

## Supervision is an operating responsibility

The responsible broker must maintain reasonable supervision over transactions and personnel. This includes reviewing documents, establishing trust fund procedures, monitoring advertising, maintaining records, preventing discrimination, and making sure personnel understand their authority. An office manual that nobody implements is not effective supervision. Nor does an independent-contractor agreement eliminate the broker's duties under licensing law.

A broker can assign operational tasks to qualified people, including a branch manager where applicable. Delegation does not automatically eliminate the responsible broker's accountability. A salesperson should elevate unusual contract language, trust shortages, disputed releases, suspected fraud, and demands to conceal defects. These are matters for timely professional judgment, not problems to hide until closing.

The salesperson normally receives compensation for licensed work through the responsible broker. A seller cannot bypass that relationship by paying the salesperson a separate negotiating fee directly. Referral compensation also requires attention to licensing and the service performed. A broker cannot pay an unlicensed person for performing acts that required a license simply because the payment occurs after closing.

## Assistants can support, but cannot replace, licensees

The distinction between administrative work and licensed work is functional. An assistant may organize files, schedule inspections, obtain status information, deliver approved materials, and perform permitted document preparation under supervision. An assistant may check whether signatures are present; the licensee makes the final determination about compliance and advises the client about consequences.

An assistant cannot independently explain which counteroffer to accept, negotiate price, solicit a particular listing through substantive persuasion, or interpret an agreement for a buyer. At an open house, handing out prepared brochures and arranging contact with the licensee differ from conducting a property showing or answering negotiating questions. A broker's presence does not transform prohibited conduct into authorized conduct.

Consider a caller asking when an inspection report will arrive. The assistant can obtain its delivery status. If the caller asks whether the report justifies canceling the purchase, the assistant should transfer the question to a licensee. This boundary remains the same whether communication occurs by telephone, text, social media, or a website chatbot. Technology does not acquire a license by automating the conversation.

## Initial qualification and renewal

Salesperson applicants ordinarily must be at least 18 and complete the required prelicense education, including Real Estate Principles, Real Estate Practice, and an approved additional course, before satisfying examination and licensing requirements. Required practice-course content includes fair housing and implicit bias. Honesty and disclosure in the application process matter independently of a passing score; fingerprints and background review are part of licensing.

California real estate licenses generally run for four years. Renewal ordinarily requires 45 hours of DRE-approved continuing education completed within the applicable period. For a salesperson's first renewal, current requirements include specified courses in ethics, agency, trust funds, risk management, fair housing with an interactive component, and implicit bias, plus minimum consumer-protection coursework. Broker requirements and later renewals have different mandatory-course arrangements. A salesperson should not apply the broker's first-renewal list without checking the correct category.

Late renewal may be available within two years after expiration, but that opportunity does not authorize licensed activity during the lapse. Limited exemptions and extensions have their own requirements. The practical lesson is to distinguish eligibility to renew from present authority to practice, and initial education from continuing education.

## Records make conduct reviewable

Business and Professions Code section 10148 generally requires brokers to retain transaction records for three years from closing or, if a transaction is not consummated, from the listing date. The statutory categories include listings, receipts, canceled checks, trust records, and other documents obtained or executed in connection with transactions requiring a license. Special laws or circumstances may justify or require longer retention.

Electronic records must remain accessible, accurate, and reproducible for examination. Keeping only the final contract can omit important offers, instructions, disclosures, and evidence of delivery. Deleting a message because the application labels it a chat does not eliminate its significance to a transaction. A defensible file tells the story of what was represented, authorized, received, and delivered.

## Discipline and financial recovery serve different purposes

DRE licenses and regulates real estate professionals and can investigate, impose appropriate administrative sanctions, suspend or revoke licenses, and issue orders concerning unlicensed activity. Grounds can include fraud, substantial misrepresentation, trust fund violations, and failure of supervision. An ordinary commission disagreement does not automatically establish a disciplinary violation, and a DRE complaint is not a substitute for a civil damages action.

The Consumer Recovery Account, historically called the recovery fund, provides limited compensation for eligible actual, direct losses involving licensee fraud or trust fund conversion in transactions requiring a license. The claimant must satisfy statutory procedures, ordinarily including a qualifying final judgment, award, or order and collection requirements. The current maximum is $50,000 per transaction and an aggregate $250,000 per licensee. Multiple victims do not each receive an unlimited separate transaction cap. An application generally must be filed within one year after the qualifying determination becomes final. Payment can trigger license suspension until statutory repayment requirements are met.

## Worked scenario

Morgan's license expired yesterday. A renewal application is being prepared, and the broker asks Morgan to negotiate a buyer's counteroffer while an unlicensed assistant handles the open house alone. Neither instruction solves the authorization problem. Morgan must stop licensed work during the lapse; the assistant must remain within permitted administrative duties. The broker should reassign negotiations and licensed showing work to properly authorized personnel, document the arrangements, and correct the office's expiration tracking.

If a later dispute arises, complete records let the broker distinguish a communication error from unauthorized activity. A client's loss would not automatically qualify for the Consumer Recovery Account: the claimant must show the specific statutory basis and satisfy the recovery process.

## Exam review

- Identify the licensed act before analyzing a worker's title or payment method.
- A salesperson acts through the responsible broker; supervision cannot be waived by contract.
- Clerical delivery and status reporting differ from negotiation and advice.
- A renewal opportunity is not permission to practice with an expired license.
- DRE discipline, civil remedies, and Consumer Recovery Account payments are separate processes.
